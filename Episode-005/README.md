## The Script

Hemingway's Suitcase
by Chas Holloway
(DRAFT: 3-4-2025)
417 words

It was Paris in the 1920s.  The Great War had just ended, and the art scene raged.  Picasso and Braque invented cubism there.  Miro, Dali and Chagall fused the emerging styles into what was called, “L’Ecole de Paris” “The School of Paris.”

Paitings were in vogue in the cafes and salons and gallieries of Montparnasse, a southern region of the city.  In Paris, in the 1920s, innovative artists developed Fauvism, Cubism, Surrealism, and they created Dadaism, a fashionable anti-logic protest against the bourgeois, just like the post-modernist movement today. 

The revolution spread beyond painting and sculpture.  Soon, the great writers joined in.  They called themselves, “The Lost Generation,” and lamented the aimlessness and confusion caused by the slaughter of an entire decade of young men in the so-called “Great War.” 

F. Scott Fitzgerald wrote there.  So did Marcel Proust, Andre Gide, James Joyce, Ezra Pound, Gertrude Stein, and …. a youthful Ernest Hemingway.
	
Hemingway had been an ambulance driver in the war, and he absorbed Paris into his cells.  Working as a news correspondent for the Toronto Star, he wrote his very first stories there.  

In 1922, the paper sent him on assignment to Switzerland.  It turned out to be a big opportunity for the young Hemingway.  The Star editor there was impressed with his writing and asked to see more.  Hemingway contacted his wife, Hadley, back in Paris, and she packed all his manuscripts into a suitcase to bring them, at once.  

During the journey, in Lyon, she stepped off the train just for a moment — to buy a bottle of Evian water for the long trip.  When she got back to her seat, Hemingway’s suitcase was gone.  

In a letter to Ezra Pound, Hemingway wrote, “I went to Paris to see what was left.  Hadley had made the job complete by packing all my carbons, duplicates, everything.”  She’d even packed all his handwritten notes for a novel in progress.  

The suitcase which contained all his early short stories, his first Nick Adams stories, and his first novel, is still missing today.  

It’s one of the great art mysteries of all time — what happened to Hemingway’s suitcase?

Hemingway offered a reward.  He bought a classified ad in the newspaper — and offered ten dollars.  That was all he could afford at the time.  

If you can find the suitcase, you might still collect the ten dollars — along with the $100 million, or so, you’d get at a Sotheby’s auction.

I’m Chas Holllway for Thinkopolis.

END
